Shitou Irrigation Canal

Home Nearby Attractions Shitou Irrigation Canal

Japanese-era Water Conservancy Heritage · A Field Museum of Hakka Water-Respect Culture

Lion-Head Irrigation is in Shishan Village, Meinong District, Kaohsiung City—an important Japanese-era water conservancy project built by Hakka ancestors to irrigate Meinong Plain farmland, at an elevation of about 150 meters. This irrigation follows Lion's Head Peak Trail's mountain foot winding along. Local elders regard it as the "lion tail" in the "lion-form protection" feng shui legend, echoing the "lion head" on the summit. With wild ginger lilies blooming along the canal and abundant water ferns, it is Hanyuan B&Ba recommended private photography secret and cultural walking route.

This seemingly ordinary irrigation carries the wisdom and sweat of Japanese-era Hakka ancestors—the most intuitive entry to understanding Meinong's "respect heaven and cherish water" culture. Walking along the canal, you'll see century-old masonry craftsmanship, lush canal-side plants, and the solemn atmosphere of Bogong shrines. Every section, every stone, every flower has its own story waiting for you.

1. Historical Origins and Hakka Water-Respect Culture

Lion-Head Irrigation's excavation dates to the Japanese-era Taisho period. Meinong was an important rice and tobacco production area in Kaohsiung Prefecture. The Xiadanshui River (today's Gaoping River) had abundant water, but Meinong Plain sits at the foothills, heavily reliant on mountain water sources. The Japanese planned masonry irrigation at Lion-Top Mountain, channeling upstream mountain streams into the plain, total length over 2.5 km. The challenging project mobilized many Hakka villagers, regarded as an important milestone in Meinong water conservancy history. The irrigation not only solved farmland irrigation but also formed a complete paddy settlement system with Meinong's tobacco barns and rice drying grounds—concrete manifestation of Hakka ancestors' spirit of "respecting heaven, cherishing water, diligent farming-study."

Hakka culture has deep respect for "water," originating from cherishing water sources when crossing to Taiwan for pioneering. Along Lion-Head Irrigation, you can still see multiple small Bogong shrines (Earth God temples) and water-head altars, built by ancestors praying for abundant canal water and peaceful fields. Every lunar spring planting, local residents hold the "Water-Head Ceremony" led by village elders with incense, wine, and gold paper offerings, praying for stable water and abundant harvests. This "water respect" culture can be seen in other Meinong irrigation too, but Lion-Head Irrigation, with the most complete preservation and highest density of Bogong shrines, is regarded as an important field area for studying Hakka water conservancy customs.

After the war, the irrigation was managed by the Farmland Irrigation Association. Though no longer the main water supply due to irrigation technology advancement, the exquisite masonry craftsmanship and rich canal-side ecology led local cultural history groups from the 2000 s onward to promote irrigation cultural walks and ecology interpretation. Walking along the canal, visitors can observe two common masonry techniques: "herringbone masonry" and "random masonry," with kidney ferns, cliff brake ferns, and lace ferns growing on canal walls, clear water and swimming fish at the canal bottom, forming a natural "Water Conservancy Museum" and "Ecology Classroom." For depth travel lovers, Lion-Head Irrigation is more than a scenic spot—it's an important text for reading Meinong Hakka culture.

Local elders' oral history tells a moving story: when excavating the irrigation, the project was repeatedly interrupted by landslides and creek flooding. Villagers sought help from Bogong at the foot of Lion-Top Mountain; after three days of fasting and prayer, it was successfully completed. To thank Bogong's blessing, villagers built the "Water-Head Bogong Shrine" at the irrigation source and agreed to hold the "Water Thanksgiving Festival" every lunar February 2 (Bogong's birthday). Though the legend is difficult to verify completely, it reflects Hakka ancestors' belief in "human-nature harmonious coexistence" and makes Lion-Head Irrigation more than a water project—carrying villagers' collective memory and cultural identity. Today, the "Water Thanksgiving Festival" is one of Meinong's important Hakka folk activities, attracting cultural researchers and visitors.

2. Location and Transportation

Lion-Head Irrigation is in Shishan Village, Meinong District, Kaohsiung City, at an elevation of about 150 meters; east side adjacent to Lion's Head Peak Trail, west side connects to Shuangxi Arboretum and Meinong town; south side leads to Meinung Folk Village. The whole irrigation winds between low foothills and the plain with gentle terrain and minimal elevation change. The irrigation itself is a "green corridor" for local residents' irrigation; along the route, shade is dense and ecology is rich—one of the few private attractions in Meinong combining cultural, historical, and ecological values. From Hanyuan B&BRecommended route:

  1. Self-Drive: From the B&B along Wugu Street to Highway 28 highway, turn into Shishan Village industrial road; it takes about 8 至 10 minutes to reach the "Lion-Head Irrigation Entrance" marker. Set GPS to "Lion-Head Irrigation."
  2. Scooter or Bicycle: From Meinong's Zhongzheng Road to Shishan Road, connecting to Lion Top Trailhead forms a loop route—a popular cycling half-day route.
  3. Walking: FromHanyuan B&BWalking approx. 35 minutes, enjoying paddy and tobacco field scenery. We recommend departing early morning or evening to avoid midday sun.

The main irrigation trail is about 2.5 km, with dirt and stone slab paths—gentle and easy, suitable for families, seniors, and mobility-impaired visitors. Two rest pavilions, three Bogong shrines, and one century-old masonry sluice gate interpretation board along the route. No public restrooms due to mountain setting—please prepare at Hanyuan B&B or Meinong town before visiting. The entire trail is free and open daily from 06:00 至 to 18:00; no nighttime entry to protect ecology and safety.

Some sections are adjacent to industrial roads. Self-driving visitors can park free on both sides of the "Lion-Head Irrigation Marker" for about 10 small cars. We recommend weekdays or non-holidays for easier parking. If full on holidays, park at Lion Top Trailheadparking lot, then walk 5 minutes to reach the irrigation start—two attractions conveniently linked. By public transport, take Qimei Express or Kaohsiung Bus from Kaohsiung city to Meinong, then transfer to taxi or rented bicycle; it takes about 8 min.

Recommended items: non-slip sneakers, sunscreen, water, mosquito repellent, notebook, camera (close-up lens recommended), and lightweight rain gear. Some sections have no shade—bring sun hat or raincoat in summer; winter mornings are cooler—bring a light jacket. Overall, Lion-Head Irrigation is a low-difficulty attraction suitable for all ages—families, seniors, photographers, and cultural researchers can all find their own touch.

3. Highlights and Routes

Walking Lion-Head Irrigation, we recommend starting from the "Lion-Head Irrigation Marker" and visiting these 6 must-see highlights in order; each carries different historical stories and ecological features. Slow down and savor:

  • Century-old Masonry Sluice Gate: A representative Japanese-era masonry craft using "herringbone masonry" technique; still functional for water regulation today—the entry to understanding Hakka water conservancy craft.
  • Water-Head Bogong Shrine: Small Earth God temple at the irrigation source; the core of local "water respect culture" where the pre-spring "Water-Head Ceremony" is held.
  • Wild Ginger Lily Secret: Every year 6 至 9 months, white wild ginger lilies bloom along the canal with rich fragrance—a private spot for photographers. Visit early morning to catch dewdrops.
  • Water Fern Observation Area: Canal walls and stone crevices are covered with water ferns, kidney ferns, cliff brake ferns—a natural classroom for low-mountain ecology.
  • Masonry Retaining Wall Viewpoint: Overlooking Meinong Plain's rice waves, tobacco barns, and Hakka settlements—the best composition for "Lion-Form Protection" themes.
  • Connecting to Lion-Top Trailhead: The irrigation endpoint connects to Lion's Head Peak Trail, forming an "irrigation walk + summit" 3 至 4 -hour complete route.

Recommended: Morning 8 arrive at the irrigation, first spend 1.5 hour walking along the canal, observing ecology and photographing wild ginger lilies. At noon, Hanyuan B&BEnjoy Hakka cuisine and rest; afternoon 3 heading toLion's Head Peak summit for sunset and night view—forming a journey blending culture, ecology, and hiking. With more time, combine the irrigation with Meinung Folk Village、Shuangxi Arboretumcombined to form a complete 'Meinong One-Day Tour' route.

Advanced play for photography lovers: bring a wide-angle lens to shoot the irrigation's winding S shapes, and use morning fog or evening golden light to create a "Hakka water town" atmosphere. For ecology macro photography, focus on canal wall water ferns, kidney ferns, lace ferns, and blooming wild ginger lily close-ups—often stunning results. In autumn-winter when water levels drop, you can clearly photograph the masonry sluice gate's structural details—an excellent historical photography theme.

4. Seasonal Highlights and Ecology

Lion-Head Irrigation has different scenery each season—a must for nature and photography lovers. Water levels, temperatures, and plants vary by season; choose the best time for your interests:

  • Spring (Mar–May): Taiwan wild lilies, Taiwan stone楠, and chinaberry flowers bloom along the canal—great flower-viewing season. Spring planting with abundant water lets you see farmers irrigating traditionally.
  • Summer (Jun–Sep): Wild ginger lily bloom season; the whole canal is filled with rich ginger fragrance. Early morning 5 至 7 and dusk 5 至 7 are the best shooting times; photographers often come for compositions.
  • Autumn (Oct–Nov)：水圳水量減少，可清楚觀察砌石水閘門的構造細節；Autumn-winter 之際也是賞鳥季節，常可見白鷺鷥、翠鳥and紅尾伯勞在水圳覓食。
  • Winter (Dec–Feb)：水圳水量穩定低水位，可仔細觀察圳底的生態，包括台灣鬥魚、沼蝦and高體鰟鮍等原生魚類。

圳畔生態觀察重點：除了野薑花外，還可見水蕨、腎蕨、車前草、倒地蜈蚣等濕地植物；鳥類方面則以白鷺鷥、翠鳥、紅尾伯勞為主；夜間還有機會觀察到螢火蟲、蛙類and夜鷺。建議攜帶望遠鏡and微距鏡頭，輕聲漫步以免驚擾野生動物，共同維護這片珍貴的淺山生態。

特別推薦的「野薑花晨拍」and「砌石夕照」兩個攝影主題：前者建議清晨 5 點半抵達，捕捉露珠凝結在白色花瓣上的瞬間，背景以淡藍晨霧為襯托，畫面清新動人；後者建議evening 4 點半抵達，利用斜射光線打在砌石圳牆上的溫暖色澤，呈現水圳的歷史厚度and人文美感。這兩個時段也是避開正午烈日的最佳 walk 時光，建議攝影愛好者and一般遊客都能參考。

5. Tips and Practical Info

  • 水圳部分路段濕滑，建議穿著防滑運動鞋or登山鞋，避免穿拖鞋or涼鞋。
  • 請勿戲水、垂釣or丟擲石頭，以維護水質and生態。
  • 請勿採摘野薑花、水蕨or任何植物，保留給下一位訪客。
  • 步道全段無遮蔭也無公廁，建議於Hanyuan B&BorMeinong Town Center先完成補給。
  • No entry at night to avoid accidents.
  • 請勿餵食野生動物or丟棄垃圾，共同維護水圳環境。
  • 請尊重伯公壇and在地信仰，勿喧嘩、勿跨越神壇。
  • 夏季 6 至 9 月為野薑花盛開期，建議清晨 5 至 7 heading to拍攝，光線最佳且氣溫涼爽。
  • 水圳屬於農田水利設施，請勿破壞砌石牆面and水閘門，共同維護珍貴文化資產。

7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Shitou Irrigation Canal全長多長？ walk 一圈需要多久時間？

獅子頭The main irrigation trail is about 2.5 公里, at an elevation of about 150 公尺，平緩好走，一般遊客輕鬆 walk takes about 1.5 至 2 hours; we recommend 預留 2.5 小時以利拍照and觀察生態。沿途設有兩處休憩涼亭，可隨時停下休息。建議攜帶飲水、防曬用品and防蚊液，並穿著防滑運動鞋以策安全。

Shitou Irrigation Canal最佳觀賞野薑花的季節是何時？

野薑花盛開期為每年 6 to 9 月，其中 7 至 8 月清晨 5 至 7 點花香最濃郁，是攝影愛好者公認的黃金拍攝時段，建議穿著防滑鞋並攜帶防水袋保護相機。除野薑花外，夏季還可見水蕨茂盛、蛙類活躍，是觀察濕地生態的最佳時節。

Shitou Irrigation CanalandLion's Head Peak Trail有關聯嗎？

兩者地形相連，Shitou Irrigation Canal沿著Lion's Head Peak山腳蜿蜒而過，當地Hakka先民將Lion's Head Peak視為獅身、水圳視為獅尾，整體構成「獅形守護」風水傳說，可From水圳步道接駁至Lion Top Trailhead。建議Morning 走水圳、下午攻Lion's Head Peak，是兼具文化and登山樂趣的經典半日遊route。
