Zhuzimen Power Plant

Home Nearby Attractions Zhuzimen Power Plant

Taiwan's First Hydropower Plant · National Monument · A Century-old Baroque Architecture Marvel

Zhuzi-Men Power Plant is in Zhuzi-Men, Meinong District, Kaohsiung City—the most iconic building in Taiwan's electric power history. In 1909 (Japanese era Meiji 42) completed, Taiwan's first hydropower plant, 20 years earlier than large plants like Sun Moon Lake. The main structure is a three-story Baroque brick building, with red brick, white trim, and elegant arched windows. Inside, original 1919 -year-old Siemens generator units are still preserved. Now revitalized by Taipower as a free exhibition hall. For guests staying at Hanyuan B&B , this is a must-visit national monument when planning a Meinong cultural history trip.

1. Zhuzi-Men Power Plant History and Cultural Background

Zhuzi-Men Power Plant history dates to Meiji 35 (1902 ), when the Japanese, to modernize Kaohsiung city, planned hydropower facilities on Laonong Creek and Qishan Creek tributaries for street lighting and public facilities. After multiple surveys, they chose Zhuzi-Men in Meinong District for its abundant water, steep gradient, and proximity to Qishan Sugar Factory (Qitang Cultural Park) and Kaohsiung city power transmission routes. Construction began in 1907 , designed and supervised by engineer Nakajima Eiji of the Taiwan Governor-General's Civil Affairs Bureau, in collaboration with German Siemens.

1909 (Japanese era Meiji 42), the power plant was completed and named "Zhuzi-Men Power Station." The installed capacity was 600 kW—the first and largest hydropower plant in Taiwan, mainly supplying Kaohsiung street lighting, sugar factories, military facilities, and some civil use. The first decade after launch was the enlightenment period of Taiwan's electric power industry; many local engineers came to learn hydropower technology, making it a cradle of Taiwan's electric power talent.

1919 , to meet growing Kaohsiung demand, the Japanese built a second machine room east of the original plant, importing two new Siemens generators, raising total capacity to about 1,500 kW. After the war, the plant was taken over by Taiwan Power Company and renamed "Zhuzi-Men Power Plant," continuing its supply role for Kaohsiung. In 1970 s, as large hydropower plants (like Sun Moon Lake pumped storage) were completed, Zhuzi-Men's role was gradually replaced, and in 1983 it was officially retired.

After retirement, Taipower preserved the building and equipment and applied for monument designation. In 1992 , Zhuzi-Men Power Plant was designated a "Grade 2 Historic Site in the Taiwan-Fujian Area." In 2018 , the Ministry of Culture upgraded it to a "National Monument"—one of the few cases in Taiwan where an industrial facility became a national monument. In 2015 , Taipower further renovated the main building into the "Zhuzi-Men Power Plant Exhibition Hall," opening it to the public with volunteer docents, helping people understand Taiwan's electric power history and Japanese-era industrial architecture.

Zhuzi-Men Power Plant's architectural design was jointly completed by Siemens engineers and Japanese architects—a rare early Japanese-era industrial building blending European Baroque and Japanese structure. The main three-story red-brick building features white trim, arched windows, and a Western-style wooden truss roof, unique in Taiwanese architecture of the time. Generators, control panels, and distribution equipment were mostly German-imported, making it one of Taiwan's most complete early hydropower facilities—praised as "a living textbook of Taiwan's electric power history."

2. Location and Transportation

Zhuzi-Men Power Plant is in Zhuzi-Men, Meinong District, Kaohsiung City, administratively under Fu'an Village. Geographic coordinates are approximately east longitude 120.555 °N, 22.915 degrees, elevation about 70 meters. The plant sits next to a Qishan Creek tributary, using the water drop for power generation. From Hanyuan B&B , head north along Highway 28 (Qiping Road) for about 3.5 km; it takes only 8 至 10 minutes—one of the closest national monuments to the B&B.

By Car: From Meinong town, take Zhongshan Road to Zhongzheng Road to Fu'an Road; you'll see the entrance marker before Fu'an Bridge. Set GPS to "Zhuzi-Men Power Plant" or "National Monument Zhuzi-Men Power Plant." A small parking lot in front offers free parking for cars and scooters.

Public Transit:Take Kaohsiung city bus 8032、8046 to "Meinong Station," then take a taxi or rented scooter to the plant; it takes about 10 minutes. From HSR Zuoying Station, take shuttle bus to Qishan Bus Terminal, then transfer to a Meinong-bound bus, and walk or rent a vehicle to the plant.

By Scooter/Bike: 由 Hanyuan B&B cycle takes about 15 至 20 minutes through the Meinong Plain paddy field scenery—one of the most popular cycling routes.

Suggested duration: including the main building exterior tour, generator exhibition area, and Taipower exhibition hall cultural exhibition, takes about 1 至 1.5 hours. We recommend visiting in the morning to avoid midday crowds, and combining with nearby ChiShan Sugar Factory、Chiwei Mountain as a half-day itinerary.

Transportation around Zhuzi-Men is convenient. The Qishan Creek bike path was completed recently; visitors can ride from the plant to nearby ChiShan Sugar Factory and Qishan Old Street, total about 6 km, enjoying Qishan Creek waterfront scenery and Hakka settlements—an excellent choice for a Meinong cultural cycling trip.

3. Highlights and Routes

The core of visiting Zhuzi-Men Power Plant is "view the architecture × view the machines × learn the history." Four must-see highlights:

  1. Baroque Main Building Exterior: Three-story red-brick building with white trim and arched windows—a representative early Japanese-era public building. Photograph the full front view; red brick walls against blue sky and white clouds evoke strong European charm.
  2. German Generator Exhibition: The first-floor exhibition area preserves 1919 -year-old Siemens generator units and control panels—the only existing, most representative early hydropower generator physical exhibition in Taiwan. On-site docents explain the power generation principles.
  3. Taipower Exhibition Hall Cultural Exhibition: The second and third floors are Taipower cultural exhibition areas, displaying Taiwan's electric power history, Japanese-era electric industry materials, old photos, and early electrical appliances—an educational palace suitable for families.
  4. Volunteer Docent Tours: Volunteer docents serve on Saturdays, Sundays, and national holidays. We recommend booking a tour in advance with Taipower to deeply understand the plant's full story from construction to retirement to preservation.

Classic Tour Route: At the entrance, first appreciate the main building exterior and take photos → Enter the first-floor generator exhibition area to see German equipment → Learn hydropower principles through docent explanations → Walk up to the second and third floors for the Taipower cultural exhibition → Walk the dam ruins and canal trail behind the plant → Afterwards, visit nearby Fu'an Village Hakka settlement and tobacco field scenery.

In-depth Cultural Route: For visitors interested in Taiwan's electric power history and monument preservation. We recommend booking a volunteer docent tour in advance with Taipower to access generally closed areas like the machine room, control room, and dam top. The tour takes about 2.5 hours. Through in-depth docent explanations, you can learn the full history from 1909 completion, expansion, postwar takeover, retirement, monument designation to 2018 upgrade to national monument—the best way to learn Taiwan's electric power cultural heritage.

親子教育route：For families with kids, take photos at the main building exterior first, then learn hydropower principles from volunteer docents, and explore Taiwan's electric power development through interactive models on the second-floor Taipower exhibition. Finally, walk the dam and canal trail behind the plant to witness hydropower facilities in action—the best educational choice.

No matter which route, Zhuzi-Men Power Plant offers rich experiences blending architectural aesthetics, industrial history, and ecological culture. Adjust based on fitness, time, and group size; bring water, rain gear, and sunscreen for a smooth trip.

Fu'an Village near Zhuzi-Men preserves complete Hakka settlement scenery, including tobacco barns, Bogong (Earth God) worship centers, and traditional Hakka Sanheyuan. While visiting the plant, walk to Fu'an Village to experience traditional Hakka architecture and taste authentic Hakka treats like Lei Cha, ban tiao noodles, and red-cooked duck. Fu'an is also an important Kaohsiung tobacco-producing area. Every 6 至 8 , during the tobacco harvest season, you can see the traditional scene of smoke rising from tobacco barns—an essential part of understanding Meinong Hakka culture.

4. Seasonal Highlights and Ecology

Zhuzi-Men Power Plant's seasonal highlights focus on "solar-term activities × and natural scenery":

  • Spring (Mar–May): Tobacco fields in Fu'an Village start seedlings, alongside rice paddy transplanting—lush green scenery. The park holds energy-saving exhibitions around Earth Day, ideal for families.
  • Summer (Jun–Aug): Strong southwest monsoon brings high water levels in Qishan Creek behind the plant—powerful roaring sounds—perfect for photographing hydropower facilities. The park holds "Taipower Energy Saving Month" events with guidebooks and souvenirs.
  • Autumn (Sep–Nov)：電廠Surroundings欒樹開花轉紅，搭配福安里的菸樓景觀，是Meinong 最具代表性的秋日風情。此季節亦適合evening 時分於電廠前 walk ，感受秋日微涼的田園氣息。
  • Winter (Dec–Feb): The red-brick main building under winter sunlight is highly atmospheric—photographers' favorite. The park also hosts "Monument Sketching" events inviting local artists and students to create.

Ecology: The Qishan Creek watershed around Zhuzi-Men has rich riparian ecology. The dam and canal trail behind the plant host little egret, cattle egret, and black-crowned night heron; kingfishers and five-colored birds are common at the forest edge. Every 10 to next year 3 , the kapok trees around the park bloom with bright red flowers, contrasting beautifully with the red-brick building—a "hidden" photo hotspot for photographers.

The Qishan Creek watershed behind Zhuzi-Men Power Plant is an important habitat for many amphibians and waterbirds. Every 4 至 9 at night, you can see Asian grass frogs, tiger frogs, and black-spined toads in paddies and irrigation channels behind the plant, with fireflies dancing in a dreamy scene. Fu'an Village also preserves complete Hakka farmland scenery—tobacco fields, rice paddies, and betel nut groves—an excellent place to understand Meinong Hakka culture and natural ecology.
