Shuangxi Arboretum

Home Nearby Attractions Shuangxi Arboretum

Japanese-era Forestry Trial Site · 97 tropical tree species, an ecological treasure trove

Shuangxi Arboretum is in Shuangxi, Meinong District, Kaohsiung City, established during the Japanese era in 1935 as a tropical forestry trial site covering about 7.65 hectares, still preserving 97 tropical and subtropical tree species, including mahogany, teak, flame tree, Indian rosewood, ironwood, and acacia. The shaded trails are gentle with beautiful forest scenery—Hanyuan B&Brecommended top pick for family outings, ecological education, birdwatching, and morning walks.

This seemingly quiet arboretum is actually a "living forestry museum" and "forest ecology classroom." Almost every tree has a number and an information board recording species, origin, introduction year, and growth story—one of the few places in Taiwan to closely observe 90 years of tropical forestry trial results. Whether you love birdwatching, photography, hiking, or family outings, you'll have a fulfilling and intellectual morning here.

1. Historical Origins and Forestry Cultural Background

Shuangxi Arboretum's history dates back to 1935 (Showa 10 ), when the Japanese government, responding to the southward policy and tropical forestry development needs, planned a tropical forestry trial site in the Shuangxi area of Meinong Village, Qishan County, Kaohsiung Prefecture. Led by the Taiwan Governor-General's Forestry Research Institute, precious tropical tree species were introduced from Southeast Asia, India, and Africa, including teak (Tectona grandis, native to India and Myanmar), mahogany (Swietenia macrophylla, native to Central and South America), flame tree (Delonix regia, native to Madagascar), and Indian rosewood (Dalbergia sissoo, native to the Indian subcontinent). These economically valuable timber species were studied to see if they could be widely planted in central-southern Taiwan hills.

After the war, Shuangxi Arboretum was managed by the Forestry Research Institute, successively under the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, and the Council of Agriculture's Forestry Research Institute. It still maintains research and educational functions today. Almost every tree has a number and information board recording species, origin, introduction year, and growth status—one of the few places in Taiwan to see in full 1930 -era forestry trial history as a living museum. After nearly 90 years, many species have grown into giant trees, providing shade and cool climate—a precious green asset for Meinong.

Beyond tropical economic species, Shuangxi Arboretum preserves many native Taiwan species, such as acacia, camphor, sweet gum, and Chinese hackberry, forming diverse "exotic and native" coexistence. This design serves forestry trial needs but also creates a rich ecological environment—the dense canopy provides habitat for birds, butterflies, and insects, while the humus and humidity nurture ferns and fungi, making Shuangxi one of Meinong's most important ecological education sites. Every spring and fall, the Forestry Research Institute and local Meinong community co-host "Shuangxi Arboretum Ecology Walks," attracting many families and ecology enthusiasts.

Notably, during the Japanese era, Shuangxi Arboretum also served as a "tropical forestry promotion demonstration." After successful trials, seedlings were promoted to afforestation in Kaohsiung and Pingtung plains, becoming an important timber source for postwar Taiwan's furniture and construction industries. Today's teak and mahogany forests can be called "living textbooks" of Taiwan's forestry history—important for studying tropical forestry, wood processing, and furniture industry development. Recently, the Forestry Research Institute has actively promoted the "Arboretum Digital Archive" project, recording each tree's number, species, age, girth, and growth photos into a digital database, and opening up QR Code audio guides, letting general visitors learn each old tree's story through their phones.

2. Location and Transportation

Shuangxi Arboretum is in Shuangxi, Meinong District, Kaohsiung City, near Lion Top TrailheadandShitou Irrigation Canal, at an elevation of about 80 至 100 meters, with gentle terrain and pleasant climate. The park is surrounded by low foothills, with Lion-Top ridge to the north and a Meinong Creek tributary to the south, forming a beautiful "mountain, stream, and shade" environment—one of Meinong's most complete ecological green corridors. Some sections are dirt paths—wear non-slip sneakers. In summer, bring a sun hat and plenty of water. Winter mornings can be cool—bring a light jacket. Overall, Shuangxi Arboretum combines forestry knowledge, ecological education, and leisure walking. Families, seniors, birders, and teaching groups can all enjoy a fulfilling half-day.

3. Highlights and Routes

Walking the Shuangxi Arboretum loop, we recommend starting from the entrance marker and visiting these 6 must-see highlights. Each showcases different tree species and ecology. Slow down and savor this "living forestry museum":

  • Mahogany Avenue: The classic section of the loop, about 200 meters of mahogany-shaded avenue. Every 11 至 12 during the leaf-fall season presents a golden carpet view—photographers' favorite.
  • Teak Forest: Teak native to India and Myanmar has grown here into 20 -meter tall giants with gray-brown bark and large leaves—the best teaching material for tropical economic species.
  • Flame Tree Plaza: Every year 3 至 4 : Flame trees in full bloom, flowers bright red like flames among the forest—the park's most colorful seasonal sight.
  • Indian Rosewood Path: A precious species native to India, with hard wood and fine texture used for high-end furniture. Beautiful form and slender leaves.
  • Ironwood Observation Area: Ironwood common in Taiwan's mid-low elevations. Yellow flowers attract many butterflies and bees—an ecology hotspot.
  • Native Acacia Forest: Secondary forest of native Taiwanese acacia, forming an interesting contrast with exotic species—the best place to learn native Taiwanese plants.

4. Seasonal Highlights and Ecology

Shuangxi Arboretum offers different scenery and ecology each season—a must for nature lovers and photographers. Forest, bird, and insect conditions vary by season; choose the best time for your interests:

  • Spring (Mar–May): Flame tree bloom season; the whole park is dotted with bright red flowers. Acacia sprouts fresh golden buds. Ironwood yellow flowers attract many swallowtail and purple crow butterflies.
  • Summer (Jun–Sep): The park's shade is densest—a great summer escape. The plum rain season makes ferns and fungi most active—observe various mushroom ecologies.
  • Autumn (Oct–Nov): Sweet gum and Chinese hackberry begin to yellow, presenting autumn's exclusive golden canopy. Active birds make this the prime birdwatching season.
  • Winter (Dec–Feb): Mahogany leaf-fall season, the ground covered in golden leaves—the park's most iconic Instagram photo spot. Warm winter sun and dry trails make this the most comfortable walking season.

Ecology highlights: common birds include five-colored bird, Japanese white-eye, light-vented bulbul, black bulbul, Taiwan blue magpie, crested goshawk. Butterflies: swallowtail, purple crow, common grass yellow. At night, you may see owls, flying squirrels, and fireflies. Bring binoculars and a guide; walk quietly to avoid disturbing wildlife—help protect this precious ecological treasure.

Photography theme recommendation: the "flame flower sea" of flame trees against blue sky is the most iconic park photography theme. 5. Tips and Practical Info

  • Some trails are dirt paths—wear non-slip sneakers, not slippers or sandals.
  • Do not pick leaves, flowers, fruits, or any plants—leave them for the next visitor.
  • Do not feed wildlife or litter—help protect the park environment.
  • There is only one public restroom (at the entrance). We recommend preparing at Hanyuan B&B or in Meinong town before visiting.
  • No BBQ, cooking or camping in the park.
  • Do not ride scooters or bicycles onto trails—walking only.
  • No entry at night to avoid accidents.
  • Bring mosquito repellent, rain gear, and water.
  • 3 至 4 Flame tree bloom months are crowded. Visit on weekdays or mornings to avoid crowds and enjoy the best light.
  • 11 至 12 Mahogany leaf-fall months have slippery ground—wear non-slip shoes and walk carefully.

7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does Shuangxi Arboretum require tickets? What are the opening hours?

Shuangxi Arboretum is a free forestry trial site, open daily 06:00 至 18:00 . The park does not charge any ticket or parking fee. Visit during the day and bring water and mosquito repellent. The entrance has public restrooms and a small parking lot—one of the few open forestry sites in Meinong combining research, education, and leisure.

What are the must-see tree species at Shuangxi Arboretum? What is the best month to visit?

Must-see species include Mahogany Avenue, Teak Forest, Flame Tree, Indian Rosewood, Ironwood, Acacia—a total of 97 species. In 至 4 , flame trees bloom most spectacularly. In 至 12 , mahogany leaf-fall creates the golden avenue view. Each season has unique forest scenery—we recommend visiting year-round.

Is Shuangxi Arboretum suitable for kids? Are there guided tours?

The park has gentle trails and dense shade; the full loop is about 2 km—ideal for families. The park has about 50 species plaques, with QR Code audio guides available—the best outdoor ecology teaching site. The Forestry Research Institute also hosts "Little Docent" events; families and school groups are welcome to register.

Is it convenient from Hanyuan B&B to Shuangxi Arboretum? Do I need a car?

Driving from Hanyuan B&B approx. 12 minutes by car. Set GPS to "Shuangxi Arboretum." You can also cycle along the Meinong Creek bike path through scenic countryside—a popular Meinong cycling route. A free parking lot at the entrance accommodates about 20 small cars.

By public transport, take Qimei Express or Kaohsiung Bus to Meinong from Kaohsiung, alight at Meinong Station, then transfer to a taxi or rented bicycle; the full journey takes about 12 minutes. From Meinong Station, you can also walk about 25 minutes through paddy fields and Hakka settlement scenery.
